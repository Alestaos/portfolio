## What it is

A VR training concept for the Extended Reality Theory module of my MSc in Computing (Games & XR).
The trainee is a brand-new zookeeper, and the task is feeding time with a lion. The learning goal
is to work safely around the lion by watching its body language. Lions are unpredictable at
feeding time, so it's a mistake you want people to make in VR first.

## How it was made

- **Storyboard:** 23 shots across scenes 1.0 to 3.1. Every panel notes the action, the player's
  action and the sensory experience (sound, haptics, feedback).
- **Embodiment:** the keeper looks down and sees their own hands beside the carcass.
- **Weight:** dragging the carcass slows the keeper's movement, a pseudo-haptic cue for how heavy
  it is.
- **The lion's cues:** it moves closer, drops low to the ground, then prowls. A tutorial message
  tells the keeper to watch its head.
- **Failure and feedback:** zoo crowd noise draws the keeper's attention, the lion pounces, and
  the keeper can reset or open a feedback dashboard with a recorded replay of their mistakes.
- **Animatic:** the panels are timed into a 1-minute 58-second cut with music, sound effects and
  voice-over.
